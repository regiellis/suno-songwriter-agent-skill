# Electronic, Dance, And Experimental Writing

Use for groove-led tracks, dance songs, instrumentals, and texture-led forms. Decide whether the deliverable is a vocal single, club arrangement, listening piece, or cue. The same lane can support several forms. Do not automatically turn all electronic music into a pop build/drop song.

## Compose The Repeating Unit

Choose the repeating idea: bass figure, drum relationship, melodic cell, vocal fragment, or sound gesture. Identify what stays fixed and what changes over longer phrases. Repetition can make small changes meaningful; it does not need a new lyric fact every time.

Plan a few audible events: a part enters, a bass note changes, a phrase drops out, a response appears, or a texture widens. Place them at intentional phrase boundaries or use a deliberate disruption. If the listener cannot identify the core idea, adding more layers rarely clarifies it.

For a DJ-oriented arrangement, provide useful entry/exit space and enough recurring pulse for mixing. Do not require a 32-bar intro on a short vocal single. Bar counts are compositional choices and generation targets, not exact Suno commands.

## Lane Decisions

| Lane | Hook And Groove | Form / Vocal Decision | Main Failure To Check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| House | Recurring kick/bass relationship, chord or vocal figure | Choose vocal-song sections or gradual club layering | Vocal phrasing obscures the groove; every return adds unnecessary layers |
| Techno | Rhythmic/timbral cell, interlocking parts, controlled repetition | Small changes can carry long spans; vocals may be absent or fragmentary | Imposed pop bridge or festival drop defeats the hypnotic brief |
| Trance | Repeating melodic contour, harmonic tension, long anticipation | Plan the return of a recognizable motif after contrast | Constant climax leaves no meaningful return |
| Drum and bass | Breakbeat drive against bass and a slower-perceived topline | Choose liquid, dancefloor, or another specific branch; phrase the voice at the intended pulse | Singing every drum subdivision makes the lyric rush |
| Jungle | Break editing and rhythmic detail as compositional material | Give breaks and bass space to answer one another | Treating it as generic fast four-on-the-floor dance music |
| UK garage / 2-step | Skipping or swung drum placement, bass, vocal syncopation | Vocal entrances should fit the gaps; keep the requested rhythmic feel | Flattening the groove into straight house |
| Dubstep / bass music | Half-time weight, bass gesture, intentional negative space | Distinguish sparse/deep and maximal festival directions | Every gap filled; bass and lead both demand constant foreground |
| Breakbeat / electro | Syncopated drum identity and recurring bass/lead cell | Develop groove contrast without requiring tempo changes | Describing only synth timbre, leaving the rhythmic identity unspecified |
| Hyperpop / glitch pop | Topline or sound-edit hook, deliberate exaggeration | Choose which moments fracture and what survives them | Random switches erase the recognizable song |
| Trip-hop / downtempo | Repeated groove, harmonic atmosphere, intimate or detached voice | Long phrases can sit over sparse attacks | Automatically substituting generic lofi study music |
| Ambient / drone | Texture, register, harmonic field, or slowly changing motif | Decide whether pulse and vocals exist at all | Forced climax, backbeat, or verse/chorus that the brief never requested |
| Chiptune / game electronica | Compact motif, bass motion, timbral constraint | Phrase an A/B contrast and a deliberate return or loop | Nostalgic sound label without a tune or functional loop plan |

These distinctions are a starting menu. Preserve a requested departure and specify its musical consequence.

## Vocal Writing Over Dance Grooves

Decide whether the voice tells a story, acts as a recurring hook, or becomes rhythmic material. For a phrase that repeats over a bass hook, leave an answer-sized rest. For a full vocal song, reserve a clear section for narrative detail so every drop is not burdened with exposition.

A chopped-vocal texture is still a voice. Exclude it when the user wants a strictly voiceless instrumental. If words matter, keep the phrase intact at least where comprehension is required; a chopped sound should not be counted as verified delivery of a lyric.

Original repetition plan:

```text
First return: a short sung request, followed by the bass answer.
Second return: same request and rhythm, new upper harmony.
Final return: leave the first half empty; the bass suggests the missing hook,
then the voice completes it.
```

The development comes from recognition and absence. A rewritten chorus is unnecessary.

## Tension And Release

A build can increase subdivision, narrow space, delay the bass return, prolong a harmonic suspension, or raise register. A release can be a stripped groove rather than a louder stack. State the intended mechanism instead of only saying `epic drop`.

Before the return, choose a transition event: a rest, a fill, a held note, a filtered texture, or a phrase overlap. Preserve the downbeat or deliberately displace it. Avoid stacking every transition device at once.

## Texture-Led And Experimental Forms

For ambient or experimental work, define a listening trajectory: settle, destabilize, expose a detail, return altered. Change one audible dimension at a time when the intended movement is subtle. For a collage or discontinuous piece, make the discontinuity intentional and describe the recurring material that gives it identity, if any.

A non-narrative vocal can use phonetic shape, breath, repeated language, or fragmented images. Do not force a plain-speech emotional claim onto it. Still honor exact words, no-voice constraints, and requested duration.

## Fusion Checks

Choose the shared pulse before adding acoustic or regional parts. A folk phrase over 174 BPM drum and bass may keep an 87 BPM vocal feel. That is a relationship between layers, not a mandatory tempo change. For a 6/8 melody over a 4/4 beat, either rephrase the melody, specify a deliberate cross-rhythm, or use separate sections; silently calling it a blend leaves the timing unresolved.

When a bass pattern defines the dance style, let another genre contribute harmony, melody, or phrasing without masking that pattern. For exact transitions or loop boundaries, use audio review and the editing workflow in `studio-and-iteration.md`.
