# Genre Palettes

This file is a menu of suggestions, not a recipe per genre. Each lane shows where its tempo often sits, the roles that tend to carry its identity, and several real directions it can take. The brief decides which of them, if any, to use.

For writing decisions start with `genre-songwriting.md` and the family file it routes to; this file covers sound. Pair it with `style-selection.md` (lane selection, anti-lock-in) and `suno-v6-controls.md` (how to write the Style line).

## How To Use

1. Read the brief for its own specifics: place, speaker, era, language, use (a club set, a wedding, a game loop, a 90-second opening), and length. These narrow the sound more than the lane label does.
2. Pick a branch, not the bare lane. Name it in Style when it helps: `Chicago jacking house` or `deep house` says more than `house`. A real branch missing from these tables is fair to use.
3. Choose the roles: what the bass does, what carries the pulse, what sits in the foreground, and who sings in what way. The listed instruments are alternatives; take one per role or bring another. Two to four instruments usually carry a Style line.
4. Set the tempo from the phrasing and the use. A band shows where the lane often sits, not a limit.
5. Make at least one choice that comes from this brief and not from the table: an instrument tied to the setting, a tempo set by how the story moves, a form set by who is speaking.
6. Compare with earlier songs in the same lane in this conversation, persona, or album. Two songs in one lane should not share tempo, foreground instrument, vocal role, and form unless the user wants continuity (a persona, an album, a series).

Two bluegrass briefs can land far apart: a gospel quartet sung partly a cappella in slow waltz time, and a newgrass instrumental with a dobro lead, jazz chords, and a long solo section.

## Reading The Tables

- **Tempo:** BPM where the lane often sits. "Half-time feel" means drums or vocal phrase at half the counted tempo.
- **Roles:** Bass, Pulse, and Foreground name common jobs with interchangeable options, not a fixed lineup.
- **Branches And Departures:** real sub-scenes, eras, regional branches, density changes, vocal-role changes, instrumental versions, and other forms. "Often instrumental" means enable Instrumental in Advanced; keep voice exclusions out of Style.

## Reminders

- The middle of a BPM band is not the default: pick the tempo the song wants.
- For a fusion, state the actual pulse and any half-time relationship; do not average two lanes' bands (see `genre-fusion.md`).
- Do not let any one row become your house style. The skill explicitly bans defaulting to `cinematic alt-R&B`, `gospel pads`, `warm male vocal`, `piano and strings`, and that applies to every other row here too.
- Sub-genres and fusions are often better than a bare lane label. Combine deliberately, not by piling on tags (see `rule-of-3.md` § Arrangement Rule Of 3). For blending two lanes, a mid-song genre switch, or several genre versions of one song, see `genre-fusion.md`.
- For non-English lanes, match lyric phrasing to the language, not only the genre label (see `multilingual.md`).
- "Korean music" is not only K-pop. Korean ballad, K-hip hop, K-R&B, Korean indie/folk, OST, and trot are distinct lanes; ask or infer which (see `multilingual.md`).
- For anime OP/ED, TV themes, idents, trailer cuts, and game themes, see `screen-music.md` for form and edit length; these rows are only a sonic starting point.
