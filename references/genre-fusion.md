# Genre Fusion And Multiple Treatments

Use when combining styles, moving between them, or presenting alternatives. Load the relevant writing families through `genre-songwriting.md`; use `genre-palettes.md` for optional timbres. The aim is a coherent song whose influences have audible jobs, not a longer tag list.

## Identify The Assignment

| Request | Approach |
| --- | --- |
| One blended song | Allocate groove, phrasing, harmony, form, and timbre across influences |
| One song with style changes | Plan sections, transition events, and what survives each change |
| The same song in several styles | Preserve requested constants; adapt only the permitted phrasing and arrangement |
| Several uploaded sources | State which source contributes what; map to available Suno controls |

Do not assume that naming two genres means alternating them. A continuous blend, an evolving blend, and a sudden switch are different outcomes.

## Allocate Musical Roles

For a simple brief, keep this planning internal. Show a compact table only when useful to review.

| Layer | Decision | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pulse and subdivision | What grid do the performers share? | 174 BPM drums with an 87 BPM vocal feel |
| Drum and bass interaction | Which pattern carries the groove? | Breakbeats and sub; acoustic bass omitted to leave room |
| Harmony | Which cycle, voicing, drone, or modal behavior remains coherent? | Folk chord movement voiced by jazz-influenced keys |
| Lead phrasing | How does the voice or instrument enter, breathe, and land? | Conversational country lyric over spacious half-time phrasing |
| Hook | What is recognized each time? | Same sung title and banjo answering figure |
| Form | Narrative verses, chorus returns, vamp, gradual build, or another scheme? | Story verses alternating with a recurring dance refrain |
| Timbre and space | Which colors support those jobs? | Dry acoustic attacks above deep electronic bass |

One influence can supply the groove while another supplies form or phrase language. Equal influence does not require equal instrument counts. Anchor-and-accent is a useful option, not a rule that one genre must own rhythm, tempo, and structure together.

For three or more influences, assign each a distinct contribution and remove duplicates. A combination of folk narrative, garage rhythm, and orchestral response specifies musical roles; `folk garage cinematic jazz soul` leaves the relationship unresolved. Preserve all requested influences, but explain a conflict if they demand incompatible foreground roles.

## Resolve Conflicts Before Drafting

| Collision | Resolve It Musically |
| --- | --- |
| Different tempo conventions | State the actual BPM unit and perceived half/double-time relationship; do not average genre BPM bands |
| Different meters | Rephrase one part onto a shared meter, deliberately design a cross-rhythm, or separate the sections |
| Swing against straight subdivisions | Choose the subdivision for each layer and where their accents align; audition intentional tension |
| Dense lyric over a busy riff | Allocate rests, simplify the riff under words, or trade foreground phrases |
| Two bass identities | Choose one foundation, alternate them, or move one figure to a higher register |
| Moving chords against a fixed modal/drone part | Use compatible tones, alter one part, or reserve it for a different section |
| Huge ambience against intelligible fast delivery | Keep the lead clearer and place the diffuse layer behind or between phrases |
| Story development against a repeated dance hook | Give narrative detail to designated passages and preserve repetition in the groove sections |

A brief need not solve every theoretical detail in text. Resolve the conflict that would otherwise change the identity of the song. Exact execution remains an audio check.

## Worked Blend: Folk And Drum And Bass

Intent: a story about a missed train, with a recurring refrain and continuous breakbeat momentum.

- Folk contributes a specific speaker, conversational verse, and a repeated title whose meaning changes.
- Drum and bass contributes the 174 BPM breakbeat/sub foundation.
- The voice phrases at an 87 BPM feel, leaving rests; the guitar answers the voice instead of strumming continuously over every accent.
- The refrain retains its words and contour while drums gain activity. A quieter middle passage removes guitar layers but keeps the pulse.

```text
folk storytelling over 174 BPM drum and bass, conversational vocal
at an 87 BPM feel, clear title refrain, fingerpicked guitar answers
between phrases, rolling breaks and deep sub; sparse middle passage,
return to the same refrain with fuller percussion
```

The doubled numbers specify a feel relationship, not two mandatory tempos. If the lyric rushes, revise phrase placement or bars before changing the genre labels.

## Worked Blend: Cumbia And Surf Rock

Choose the cumbia branch when it matters. In this contemporary hybrid, cumbia contributes the dance groove and recurring response; surf rock contributes guitar articulation and an instrumental hook.

Write a concise vocal refrain and leave a gap for the tremolo guitar answer. Keep the bass pattern supporting the dance groove. Bring the guitar figure back at section boundaries so it acts as a hook rather than decorative reverb.

```text
cumbia sonidera with surf-rock guitar, steady dance groove,
compact lead-and-group refrain, tremolo guitar answers each phrase;
bass anchors the pulse, guitar hook returns between verses
```

A regional label does not certify authenticity. Verify the selected rhythm and instrumentation if the user wants a traditional rather than contemporary hybrid treatment.

## Worked Blend: Soul, Post-Punk, And Ambient Texture

Soul contributes the lead's emotional phrasing and a short backing answer. Post-punk contributes the repeating bass motif and restrained drum motion. Ambient texture occupies the background and expands only in the instrumental gaps.

Keep the chorus at the same tempo and approximate loudness; let the lead sustain longer while the bass motif continues. This gives the influences distinct roles without requiring a maximal chorus, three lead instruments, or a new genre every section.

## Genre Switches

Describe the arc in Style and mark its location in Lyrics or the instrumental arrangement. Preserve one or more intentional constants: singer, hook, motif, tonal center, pulse, or recurring phrase. A deliberate hard contrast can change several at once when requested.

Plan the transition itself:

1. Identify the last audible event in the outgoing section.
2. Choose a handoff: rest, fill, held tone, pickup, pivot chord, overlapping motif, or abrupt cut.
3. Specify the new pulse/feel and the entering foreground part.
4. Decide what returns later and what has changed.

For a 6/8 ballad becoming a 4/4 dance section, state whether the dotted-quarter pulse maps to the new quarter note or whether the tempo changes. Refit the vocal rhythm rather than promising that the old phrasing automatically fits.

There is no fixed one-switch limit. More switches require clearer section planning and listening checks. If Suno blends away a needed transition, use a local edit, Extend, or a supported Studio arrangement rather than piling on tags. Section cues request behavior; they do not enforce it.

## Multiple Versions

Name the constants the user wants preserved: words, melody, hook, story, or only theme. If they request identical lyrics, keep them identical and change phrase allocation or performance cues. If rephrasing is allowed, adapt cadence to each treatment and identify meaningful changes. Do not claim text alone preserves a melody from an existing recording.

Offer a small useful set when no count is given; honor a requested count. Each treatment needs its own positive Style direction and relevant exclusions. Include sliders only when useful or requested. Existing audio that needs a new treatment belongs in the Cover/editing workflow in `studio-and-iteration.md`.

## Revision And Listening Targets

Before generating, check that each requested influence changes at least one meaningful musical decision. If all versions share the same lyric rhythm and form, that can be intentional, but instrument swaps alone may underserve a request for distinct rewrites.

After hearing a take, ask which influence disappeared and which part competed with the hook. Simplify or reassign that role. Do not automatically increase Weirdness or add exclusions. Exclude only a stated constraint or observed unwanted direction. For exact-prompt comparisons, set Variety to 0 in the Advanced tab and keep the other visible controls fixed; takes still vary.
