Use when a genre should change how the song is written, not just its instrument list. Load the relevant family below and only the sections needed. Pair with `songwriting-craft.md` for lyric decisions and `genre-palettes.md` for sound options. A palette row is not a complete songwriting method.

## Write The Identity Before The Prompt

Choose the relevant decisions, not every cell for every song:

| Decision | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Version of the genre | Which scene, era, subgenre, regional variant, or intended use? |
| Recognition | Does a refrain, flow, riff, bass pattern, groove, timbre, or dramatic turn carry the hook? |
| Phrase behavior | Where do words enter, land, breathe, repeat, or answer? |
| Time and harmony | What pulse, subdivision, bass motion, harmonic cycle, or modal behavior supports that phrasing? |
| Development | Does the song need narrative progression, sectional contrast, improvisation, or gradual change? |
| Performance | Who leads, who answers, and what must remain audible underneath? |

If deleting the instrument names makes every genre version identical, revisit phrasing and form. Instruments can carry identity, but changing only the instrument list is often an incomplete treatment.

## Unknown Or Undercovered Lanes

Use a precise reference supplied by the user or consult a reliable educational, practitioner, or cultural source when details are uncertain. Identify the groove, phrase conventions, form, and context before prescribing specialist terms. Do not invent dialect, ceremonial function, tuning, or exact rhythm from a broad regional label. Offer an explicitly described hybrid if that is what the brief supports; do not claim traditional authenticity from a few tags.

Preserve a deliberate user choice even when it departs from the examples. Genre guides describe useful possibilities, not membership tests. Exact notes, accents, and forms remain generation targets to audition.
