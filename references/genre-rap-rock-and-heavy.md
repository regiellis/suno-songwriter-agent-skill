# Rap, Rock, And Heavy Writing

Use for cadence-led verses, riff-led songs, loud or dense arrangements, and subgenre-specific contrasts. Choose the relevant lane. For hybrids, retain the rhythmic job of each part through `genre-fusion.md`.

## Rap: Plan Flow Before Counting Lines

Write against an explicit pulse. Mark the important stressed words, phrase entrances, rhyme landings, and breaths privately. A bar is a unit of musical time; a line break is an editorial choice. Sixteen lines do not certify sixteen bars.

Choose a flow device for a passage: repeated rhythmic cell, alternating short and long phrases, delayed rhyme, internal rhyme chain, or a shift in subdivision. Change it where the argument or scene turns. Constant maximum density can erase emphasis.

Build a multisyllabic rhyme bank by sound and stress, then choose words that advance the thought. A technically impressive rhyme is expendable if it forces a fake pronunciation or empty claim. Put the meaningful word where the cadence gives it attention.

Original two-phrase contrast, with uppercase stress marks for private planning only:

```text
I COUNT what it COST / you COUNT who was THERE
Your NAME on the WALL / my DUST in the AIR
```

This suggests an accent relationship, not an exact notated flow. Remove diagnostic capitalization from finished lyrics unless it is intended typography. Fit the actual words to the beat or state that audio fit is pending.

## Rap Lanes

| Lane | Writing Decision | Development And Failure Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boom bap | Let a recurring drum/sample pocket frame the cadence; use internal rhyme and clear thought units | Change argument or scene across verses; do not fill each snare gap with another syllable |
| Trap | Decide how the voice works around sustained 808s and fast hat subdivisions | Alternate busy phrases with space; triplets and ad-libs are options, not mandatory decorations |
| Melodic rap | Identify where speech becomes a sustained pitch and which vowel carries it | Keep a singable hook contour; do not write a dense spoken line and simply label it melodic |
| Drill | Specify the regional or production variant; place flow against the chosen bass/drum syncopation | Avoid generic triplet-trap cadence or a compulsory violent subject |
| Grime | Use an identifiable rhythmic delivery and sharp phrase turns against the chosen electronic beat | Distinguish it from drill and trap; preserve breath and intelligible punchline landings |
| Spoken-word / jazz-rap | Let syntax cross bars while key accents and pauses anchor the thought | Do not convert every thought into a rhyming couplet or impose a sung hook |

A chorus can simplify and reset, but a hookless rap request can organize itself through recurring cadence, thematic development, beat changes, or a final payoff. Do not add a chorus solely to satisfy the output rubric.

For ad-libs, decide whether they answer, emphasize, or contradict the lead. Write only actual sung/spoken content in parentheses. Delivery instructions stay in section cues or Style.

## Rock: Write With The Riff

Decide whether the signature riff plays under the vocal, answers it, or returns between sections. Compose the vocal rhythm around the riff's active points. If both parts demand the same accents in the same register, simplify one or alternate them.

A rock chorus can lift through longer notes over a moving riff, gang response, a harmonic turn, or a change from clipped to open articulation. It need not be a pop chorus with distortion added. Preserve an instrumental hook if the lyric is secondary to it.

| Lane | Useful Form And Phrase Choice | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Punk / hardcore | Direct claim, brief sections, communal response or compact refrain | A mandatory long build or ornate language that weakens the attack |
| Pop-punk | Conversational setup into a rhythmic, repeatable hook | Treating every chorus as shouted slogans with no specific situation |
| Indie / garage rock | Characterful diction, memorable riff, deliberate roughness | Polishing away the narrator's identity |
| Post-punk | Bass-led recurrence, clipped or declamatory phrasing, tension through restraint | Solving every section with a broad anthemic lift |
| Grunge / alternative | Contrast articulation, density, harmony, or register | Assuming quiet-verse/loud-chorus is required in every song |
| Shoegaze / dream pop | Repeat a melodic contour or phonetic motif that can survive blended texture | Filling a dense sound field with detailed exposition |
| Post-rock | Transform a motif through texture and scale; vocals optional | Adding lyric sections to an instrumental development brief |

## Metal And Heavy Subgenres

First choose the branch and vocal design. Clean traditional metal, thrash, doom, death metal, black metal, metalcore, and progressive metal are not one screamed-verse/clean-chorus template.

| Lane | Compositional Focus | Phrase / Arrangement Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traditional / power metal | Riff and melodic refrain, clear phrase arcs, instrumental lead moments | Place sustained vocal peaks where the tessitura is workable; don't make every line a peak |
| Thrash | Fast rhythmic riff identity, articulated attack, tightly coordinated stops | Fit consonants to the active rhythm without making every phrase a mouthful |
| Doom / sludge | Weight, long harmonic holds, deliberate space or repetition | Budget time for decay and long phrases; faster drums are not the default lift |
| Death / black metal | Choose riff development, texture, and harsh-vocal role for the requested branch | Do not impose clean choruses; dense or blurred texture can be intentional |
| Metalcore | Define the relationship between riff, vocal contrast, and breakdown if requested | A breakdown needs a rhythmic reason; not every song requires one |
| Progressive rock / metal | Recurring motif transformed through sections, meters, or harmony | Make a return recognizable; complexity without a relationship sounds arbitrary |

For harsh delivery, write purposeful accents and breath space without promising exact vocal techniques from tags. If the user wants intelligible words, do not hide crucial exposition inside the busiest riff passage.

For odd meter, specify grouping and keep a pulse anchor. `7/8 grouped 2+2+3` gives more information than `progressive`. A new meter should support a phrase or motif, not demonstrate complexity for its own sake.

## Worked Rap / Heavy Blend

Brief: an accusatory rap verse over industrial metal, with a shouted refrain.

- Keep the drum grid stable; guitars use short repeated attacks.
- Rap occupies the gaps between guitar accents; save dense rhyme for the less busy bars.
- Refrain reduces the lyric to one central charge, delivered by a group over longer guitar sustains.
- An instrumental turnaround repeats the verse rhythm before the next entry.

```text
industrial metal with cadence-led rap verses, rigid drum-machine pulse,
clipped low guitar attacks answering the flow; shouted group refrain
with sustained guitars, same rhythmic motif through each return
```

This is a design, not proof of rendered synchronization. If the rap and riff clash in audio, adjust phrase timing or one part's activity before adding more genre tags.
